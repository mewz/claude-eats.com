steps:
  - text: Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it generously — it should taste like seawater. Keep it rolling while you build the sauce.
    why: Rigatoni takes longer than the sauce, so the water goes on first; everything else fits inside its cook time.
  - text: In a 12-inch skillet over medium heat, melt the butter. When the foam subsides, lay the sage leaves in a single layer and fry until they stop bubbling and go crisp, and the butter underneath smells nutty with brown flecks on the bottom, 2 to 3 minutes. Lift the leaves to a paper towel and hit them with a pinch of salt. Leave the brown butter in the pan.
    why: The sage is your doneness cue — the leaves crisp at the same moment the milk solids brown, so watch the leaves, not the clock. The butter they fried in becomes the base of the sauce.
  - text: Drop the rigatoni into the boiling water and set a timer for 2 minutes shy of the package al dente time.
    why: The pasta finishes cooking in the sauce, where it soaks up flavor instead of water. Pull it underdone or it will be soft by the time it hits the plate.
  - text: Turn the skillet to medium-low. Add the shallot and cook until soft, about 2 minutes, then the garlic for 30 seconds, until fragrant.
    why: Brown butter is already at the edge of burning, so the aromatics go in on gentler heat — garlic turns bitter fast in hot browned butter.
  - text: Stir in the pumpkin purée, Calabrian chili, and cream, then ladle in ¾ cup of pasta water from the pot. Simmer gently, stirring, until it loosens into a sauce that coats a spoon, 3 to 4 minutes. Grate in a little nutmeg.
    why: A few minutes in the fat takes the raw-squash edge off the purée and blooms the chili. Keep it looser than you want the finished dish — the pasta and cheese will tighten it.
  - text: When the timer goes, move the rigatoni straight from the pot into the skillet with a spider or slotted spoon, keeping the pasta water. Toss over medium heat, then add the Parmigiano a handful at a time with splashes of pasta water, tossing between each, until the sauce turns glossy and clings to every tube, 1 to 2 minutes.
    why: Starchy water, fat, and cheese emulsify into a sauce that coats instead of puddling. Adding the cheese gradually with water keeps it from seizing into clumps.
  - text: Off the heat, add the lemon zest and juice and a lot of black pepper. Taste — it should be savory first, sweet second, with the chili warm but not loud. Adjust salt, and add more Calabrian if you want it hotter.
    why: Pumpkin and brown butter both lean sweet and rich; lemon and pepper pull the dish back to savory. Acid goes in off heat so it stays bright instead of cooking off.
  - text: Plate and top with the crispy sage, pepitas, and more Parmigiano. Put the Calabrian jar on the table.
    why: Keeping the base at one tablespoon of chili lets everyone set their own heat, and the sage and pepitas give a crunch the soft sauce needs.

**Pairing.** A Grenache-led GSM or a cru Beaujolais — fruit-forward, low tannin, enough acidity to stand up to the chili. Nothing oaky.

**If the purée is the smoked batch.** Smoke and brown butter stack, so go easy on the nutmeg and keep the chili at one tablespoon in the pan. The lemon matters more here, not less.

**Purée moisture.** A loose, wet purée should cook down in the skillet for a minute or two before the pasta water goes in. A dry, long-roasted purée needs the full ¾ cup and probably a splash more at the end.

**Variations.** Brown a pound of crumbled sausage in the skillet before the sage, set it aside, and fold it in at the very end. Pappardelle or fresh egg pasta works in place of rigatoni — cut the pasta water back, since fresh pasta gives up less starch. No cream: two more tablespoons of butter and a bit more pasta water.

**Leftovers.** The sauce tightens cold. Reheat in a skillet over low with a splash of water or milk, tossing until it loosens again — the microwave makes it gummy.
