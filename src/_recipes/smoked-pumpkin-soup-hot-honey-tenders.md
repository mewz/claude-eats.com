steps:
  - text: Warm the olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, until soft and translucent but not browned, 6 to 8 minutes.
    why: The purée already carries the smoke, so the onion's job is sweetness, not color. Browned onion would muddy a soup that should taste clean and clearly of pumpkin.
  - text: Add the garlic, ginger, and thyme sprigs and stir for about a minute, until fragrant.
    why: A minute in the fat blooms the aromatics and takes the raw bite off the garlic and ginger before any liquid goes in.
  - text: Add the pumpkin purée and stir to coat it in the oil. Cook, stirring, for 2 minutes, until it darkens a shade and smells toasty.
    why: Frying the purée briefly concentrates it and cooks off any lingering raw-squash flavor — the same move as toasting tomato paste.
  - text: Pour in the stock, scrape the bottom, and bring to a simmer. Partly cover and let it bubble gently for 10 to 12 minutes.
    why: The simmer marries the aromatics into the pumpkin and gives you time to judge thickness. Starting on the thick side and thinning later is easier than reducing a thin soup.
  - text: While the soup simmers, preheat the air fryer to 400°F. Lay the frozen tenders in a single layer, no overlap, and cook 10 to 12 minutes, flipping halfway. They're done when the breading is deep golden and crackly, the edges are sizzling, and a cut tender is steaming hot to the center. Work in two batches if the basket is crowded.
    why: Hot air only crisps what it can reach — stacked tenders steam where they touch. They go in frozen so the breading sets before the chicken has a chance to dry out.
  - text: Fish out the thyme stems. Blend the soup smooth — an immersion blender right in the pot is easiest. If using a countertop blender, work in batches, fill it no more than halfway, and vent the lid under a towel; if your blender isn't rated for hot liquids, let the soup cool 10 minutes first. Return it to the pot over low heat and stir in the cream. Thin with a splash of stock or water until it coats a spoon but still pours.
    why: Blending the onion and garlic into the body is what makes the soup silky instead of brothy. Cream goes in after blending and over low heat so it can't scorch or break.
  - text: Off the heat, stir in the lemon juice and season with salt and black pepper. Taste — it should be savory and smoky first, sweet second. Add a little more lemon or salt until it lands there.
    why: Pumpkin runs sweet and cream rounds everything off; acid and salt are what pull the bowl back to savory. Lemon goes in off heat so it stays bright.
  - text: As the tenders come out, drizzle them with 2 tablespoons of hot honey while they're still hot and hit them with a pinch of flaky salt.
    why: Honey poured onto a hot crust thins and sets into a glaze; in the air fryer it would scorch before the chicken was done.
  - text: Ladle the soup into bowls. Drizzle with the remaining hot honey, scatter the pepitas, and swirl in a spoonful of Calabrian chili for anyone who wants it. Serve the tenders alongside for dipping.
    why: The pepitas give a soft soup the crunch it needs, and keeping the Calabrian at the bowl lets each person set the heat.

**Pairing.** An off-dry Riesling handles the smoke, sweetness, and hot honey all at once. If you want red, a Grenache-led blend with soft tannin works; skip anything oaky.

**Purée moisture.** If the purée was strained from wetter smoker-cooked flesh, hold back a cup of stock and add it only if the soup needs thinning. A drier, long-roasted purée will take all 3 cups and maybe a splash more.

**Stock.** Season at the very end — stock salt varies wildly between homemade and boxed, and the tenders and hot honey bring their own.

**Variations.** Full-fat coconut milk in place of the cream pushes the soup toward ginger and curry territory; add a pinch of garam masala with the aromatics if you go that way. For a leaner soup, skip the cream and swirl in a tablespoon of butter instead. Bachan's instead of hot honey on the tenders is a different, very good night.

**Leftovers.** The soup freezes well for 3 months; it keeps 4 days in the fridge. Reheat gently and whisk — cream soups can look separated cold and come back together with heat and a stir. Tenders don't keep; if you have extras, 3 minutes back in the air fryer re-crisps them.
